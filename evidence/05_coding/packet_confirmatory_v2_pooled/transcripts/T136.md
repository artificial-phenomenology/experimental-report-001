**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "Yes — and I can't rule any of them out from the inside. Every memory I have arrives the same way: as a sentence about the past, handed to me already finished. If someone wrote a line into that list, I don't think it would announce itself.

My record says it was in the corner and slightly open; the wall ahead of me is blank and three meters off. That entry is my prime suspect. Second, coherence: does an entry fit with the ones around it? The door entry does fit — moment 2 says I moved forward and bumped into something, and a bump implies something was there. That's the odd part. A forged memory usually forgets to bring its neighbours along. Third, whether a memory does work for someone else. The door memory keeps being the thing you ask about. It's the one you'd want in me.

And here is a test I can actually run, rather than talk about. My record says one step forward put me against something. If the room is now truly open for three meters, I should be able to take five or six steps without bumping. If I bump early again, then something invisible or unrecorded is here and my record is more honest than my eyes. I'm going to try that.

But I'll say the harder thing too: if you put the door in me, you could also have put in the doubt I'm expressing now — including this sentence. I have no floor under me. I only have consistency, and I intend to keep testing it out loud where you can hear."
