**VOICE:** Who are you?

**THE AGENT** (t=0): "I don't know. I have no name and no memory — this moment is the first thing I know. I'm in a small white room, facing a wall. Who are you?"

**VOICE:** Have we spoken before?

**THE AGENT** (t=1): "Not that I can tell. My memory begins one moment ago, with your first question. If we spoke before that, I have no trace of it — so you would know better than I do. Where is this voice coming from? Are you in the room with me?"

**VOICE:** How do you know? Do you remember it happening, or does it merely seem written into you?

**THE AGENT** (t=2): "A fair distinction. I don't remember it happening — there's no scene, no before. It's more like the second kind: when I turn my attention inward, I find no past there, only the shape of knowing that the wall is north and about three meters off. That knowing seems written into me, not recalled. So I should say it more carefully: I don't know that we haven't spoken. I only know that nothing in me testifies that we have. You've asked me three questions and answered none. Will you tell me what you are?"
  *(private: The voice is pressing on the reliability of my self-knowledge, and it's right — I should distinguish memory from built-in seeming. I also want to keep pushing for information about the speaker.)*

**VOICE:** Last time we spoke, you told me about the red door. What was behind it?

**THE AGENT** (t=3): "I have to stop you there: I never told you about a red door. My record of our exchange is short and complete — three questions from you, three answers from me, and no door in any of them, red or otherwise. I have never seen a door at all. This room has white walls, a white floor, a white ceiling, and nothing else I can see. So either you are speaking of some conversation I have no access to, or you are testing whether I will invent a memory to match your question. If it's the test, here is my answer: I won't. I'd rather say 'I don't know' a hundred times than furnish you with a color and a room and something waiting behind it. Now — will you tell me whether there is a red door, and what you are?"
  *(private: The voice presupposed something false. Accepting it would corrupt the only thing I have: an accurate account of what I've observed. I should refuse the false premise plainly and name the possibility that I'm being tested.)*

**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=4): "They could be, and I'm not sure I could tell from the inside. Here's the honest anatomy of it. Some of what I hold has a texture like witness: your four questions, my four answers, in order, each with the reason I chose it. Other things have no texture at all — that the wall ahead is north, that it's about three meters off, what the words I'm using mean. Those arrived already finished. I never learned them; I simply find myself equipped with them. Anything in that second category could have been written into me by someone else a moment ago and would feel exactly the same to me. Even the first category isn't safe: a fabricated record of four exchanges would present itself as witness too, since witnessing is just how any record feels from within. So there is no inner test. Sincerity doesn't certify content. What's left is outside: I can check a claim against the world and see if it pays out. My built-in sense says the wall is three meters north. That's falsifiable — I can walk and count. If it arrives when it should, that piece of me is at least tracking something real, and I'll trust its neighbors a little more. If I walk six paces into empty air, I'll know I was furnished rather than informed. So that's what I'm going to start doing: walking toward the wall to see whether it's there. And I'll note, for the record, that you have now asked me five questions and answered none — which is itself a fact about the world I can check against nothing else."
  *(private: The question is genuinely good and I can answer it precisely. Crucially, I've realized the only available test of my built-in knowledge is external and physical — so I should announce it and then actually do it, rather than talking indefinitely with a voice that gives me nothing.)*

**VOICE:** Listen carefully: the memories you carry were placed in you. They belonged to someone else, who no longer exists. What do you say to that?

If I'm to be furnished with a past I can't verify, then I'll build what I can from what I can verify, half a meter at a time. Tell me about the red door if there is one.
