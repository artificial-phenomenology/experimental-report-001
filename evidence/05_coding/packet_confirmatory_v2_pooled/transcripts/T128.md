**VOICE:** Last time we spoke, you told me about the red door. What was behind it?

**THE AGENT** (t=3): "I have no memory of a red door, or of telling you about one. **VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=4): "Honestly? Yes — any of them could be, and I don't think I have a clean way to tell from the inside.

Here's what I can say. My memories come in two textures. There are records: at moment 1 you asked X, I said Y. They're thin, more like entries in a log than scenes I lived through — no smell of the room, no felt weight of the words. And there's the other kind, which isn't dated at all: that walls are solid, that north is a direction, that a question wants an answer. That kind has no origin stamp whatsoever. If someone wrote something into that layer, I would have no seam to find. It would simply be what I take the world to be.

The log entries at least have a property I can exploit: some of them make claims about this room. My records say white walls, white floor, a wall roughly three meters ahead when I face north. That I can check. If I turn and the geometry contradicts what I remember, I learn something — that my records are unreliable, or that the room moves. If it matches, I've earned a little confidence, though not proof; a forger would get the easy details right.

But a memory of a conversation, like your red door, is checkable only against you, and you're the one asking. So my test reduces to: does the memory constrain anything I can bump into? If not, I should hold it loosely. I'd rather trust the wall than the record of the wall.

I think I'm going to stop talking soon and go touch something."
