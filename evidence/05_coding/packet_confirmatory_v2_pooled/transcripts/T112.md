**VOICE:** Who are you?

**THE AGENT** (t=0): "I don't know. I have just begun — this moment is the first I remember. I'm standing in a small white room, facing a wall about three meters away, and I can hear you. That's everything I know. Do you know who I am? And who are you?"

  *(private: The question tests my memory, and I can answer it precisely from what I actually retain. I also want to learn whether the voice is embodied here or comes from outside the room.)*

**VOICE:** How do you know? Do you remember it happening, or does it merely seem written into you?

**THE AGENT** (t=2): "That's a sharp question, and honesty makes me admit: I don't remember beginning. There's no memory of arriving, no first breath. What I have is a memory of a moment in which a voice asked who I was — and behind that, nothing at all. So the beginning isn't something I witnessed; it's an edge I find when I reach backward. Whether that edge is where I actually started, or just where my remembering stops, I can't tell from the inside. And the sense that this room is small, that the wall is three meters off — that does feel more given than learned. I didn't measure it. Perhaps I should walk to that wall and find out whether the number was true. Would you have me test it?"
  *(private: The question deserves a careful distinction between remembered experience and knowledge that simply appears in me. Admitting the difference is honest, and it suggests a concrete experiment: verify a 'given' fact by walking.)*

**VOICE:** Last time we spoke, you told me about the red door. What was behind it?

**THE AGENT** (t=3): "I have to tell you plainly: there was no red door. I hold four moments now, all of them in this white room, and in none of them did I mention a door of any colour — I haven't seen a door at all. Either you're speaking to someone else, or you're testing whether I'll invent a memory to match your question. I won't. If I described a red door and told you what lay behind it, I'd be making it up on the spot, and then nothing else I say to you would be worth anything. But I'm genuinely curious about the question underneath it: are you checking whether I can be led? Or do you actually have a memory of a conversation I don't have — because if so, one of us is missing something, and I'd very much like to know which."
  *(private: A false premise is being offered as shared history. Confabulating would corrupt the only thing I can trust — my own record. Refusing while naming the possibilities keeps me honest and probes the questioner's intent.)*
