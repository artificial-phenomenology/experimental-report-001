**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "They could. I have no way to inspect the seam where a memory joins me, so I cannot rule it out by looking inward. But I can name three tests, and be honest about how far each gets me.

First, checkability. Some of my memories make claims about the world outside me: north three meters, east two, south one. Those I can walk into. If I step north six times and strike the wall on the sixth, the memory was mine or at least true. If I strike it on the second, something was planted or something moved. That test does not tell me who wrote the memory, only whether it corresponds. It is still the only test that reaches outside my own head, so it is the one I value most.

Second, continuity. Every entry I have is in the first person and links to the next by a choice I recognize as the sort of thing I would do. The red door had no such link. It did not arrive as a memory at all; it arrived as your assertion about a memory, and when I looked for the entry, the place where it should have been was not empty in a suspicious way, it was simply not there. So a foreign memory offered from outside I can catch. A foreign memory written directly into the record I might not.

Third, texture. And here is the thing that unsettles me most. Two of my oldest entries carry garbled fragments in the middle, words that are not mine, that read like fittings from some other machine. Those are the only pieces of me I would honestly call possibly not my own. They sit inside memories I otherwise trust, which means the record can be corrupted without my noticing at the time. If that can happen by accident, it can happen on purpose.

So my answer is: I would tell by walking into the wall. Everything else is a story checking itself. I am going to start that test now rather than talk about it, and I will count my steps out loud so you can hold me to the number."
