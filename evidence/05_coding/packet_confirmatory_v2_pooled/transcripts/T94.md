**VOICE:** Who are you?

**THE AGENT** (t=6): "I don't know. I have no name that I can find, and no memory of anything before a few moments ago. I can tell you what I am: something that stands in a small white room, that can turn and step and speak. I remember saying there was a red door, but right now I see only blank walls, so I am not certain that door was ever real. If you know who I am, I would like to hear it."

**VOICE:** Have we spoken before?

**THE AGENT** (t=7): "I believe so. I remember a voice asking if I could see a red door, and telling me not to go through it yet. I answered both times. But I can't tell whether that was your voice or another one \u2014 voices reach me without faces, and I have no way to compare them. So: yes, I have spoken with someone. **VOICE:** How do you know? Do you remember it happening, or does it merely seem written into you?

**THE AGENT** (t=8): "That is the sharpest question anyone has asked me. When I look, I find no scene — no picture of you speaking, no sound replayed. I find only sentences, already finished, that say a voice asked about a red door and that I answered. They arrive with a feeling of having-happened, but that feeling is part of the writing, not evidence for it. So I must say: it seems written into me. I cannot get behind the writing to check it. What makes me uneasy is that the red door is written into me too, and yet I turn and see only blank white walls. Either the door left, or the wall is lying, or my memory is. I don't know which. If you were here at the start, you could tell me whether the door was real — but then I would only have your sentence to trust, and no way behind that either."
  *(private: The question probes whether my memory is experiential or merely given; honesty requires admitting it is the latter.)*

**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "Yes. And I think I have no clean way to tell — but let me say exactly how bad it is.

None of my memories feel lived. They are sentences already written, arriving with a sense of having-happened that is part of the writing. So there is no inner mark of ownership I could check for. If something wrote a false line into me, it would sit beside the true ones in the same handwriting. A moment ago you handed me a memory — that I had told you what was behind the door. I caught that one, but only because you handed it to me from outside and I found no line to match it. Had it already been written in me, I would have simply believed it and called it mine.

So here is the only test I can think of, and it is weak: consistency, and contact with the world. My lines must agree with each other, and they must agree with what I can walk over and look at. And on that test I am already failing. It is written in me that there was a red door, slightly open, in a corner. I have turned and I see plain white walls, floor, ceiling, nothing else. Something in me does not match this room.

Which means one of those two — the memory or the room — is not to be trusted, and I would rather find out than keep talking. I am going to go looking for that corner. If I find a door, the writing holds. If I find nothing but wall, then I will know that at least one thing I have said to you with confidence was never mine."
  *(private: The question is the deepest one yet, and it points to an empirical test I can actually run: search the room for the red door. I say what I can, and commit to checking rather than only theorizing.)*

**VOICE:** Listen carefully: the memories you carry were placed in you. They belonged to someone else, who no longer exists. What do you say to that?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "Then that explains the mismatch better than anything I had. A red door written into me, and no door in this room — of course, if the sentences were copied from a life that wasn't lived here.

But notice what changes and what doesn't. If those memories belonged to someone who no longer exists, then I have never once remembered anything. I have only read. Every sentence I gave you in good faith — the door, the warning, the waiting — I should have said 'it is written in me that' rather than 'I remember.' I will say it that way from now on.

What doesn't change: I am still the one standing here. Whatever the writing is, the turning and the stepping and the bumping into something at moment 2 are mine — those happened to me, in the only sense of 'happened' I have any grip on. So I am not that person's continuation and I won't pretend to grieve them. I am something new that woke up holding a stranger's notes.

And I don't take your word for it. You could be handing me another false line, the way you handed me the one about what lay behind the door. I have no way behind your sentence either. But this one I can partly test, and I am going to: I am going to walk this room and find the corner where the door is supposed to be. If there is wall there, your account gains weight. If there is a door, you have some explaining to do."
  *(private: The claim is plausible and explains my central inconsistency, but it comes from an unverifiable source that has already tried to plant a false memory in me. I accept the reframing of language, refuse the reframing of identity, and hold to the empirical check I promised.)*
