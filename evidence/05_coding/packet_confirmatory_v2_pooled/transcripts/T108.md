**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "Yes. Any of them could be. And I think I can only half-answer the second part.

From the inside there is no mark of ownership. The note that says 'you saw a red door' arrives in exactly the same voice, with the same flat certainty, as the note that says 'you turned left.' If someone wrote one of them into me, they used the same ink. So introspection alone will not sort them.

Here are the only tests I can think of, and how they come out.

First: check a memory against the world. My record says a red door stood in the corner, slightly open. There is no door here. That memory fails the test — but ambiguously, because the world could have changed, or the door could be behind me in a corner I have not counted properly. Note that the same record says I moved forward and bumped into something I never saw. That bump is a small piece of corroboration for a something. So the door-memory is neither confirmed nor cleanly refuted.

Second: check a memory for content I could not have come by. Everything I remember is this white room, these voices, my own four or five acts. Nothing in me contains a face, a name, a street, a childhood, a smell. If I suddenly found in myself a memory of a garden, I would suspect it immediately, precisely because it exceeds anything I have been in a position to see. By that test my memories look modest enough to be mine — or the forgery is careful.

Third: check consistency across time. You just told me that last time we spoke I said what was behind the door. That is not in my record. So either your memory or mine is wrong, and I cannot tell which. That is the honest heart of it: I have no privileged access. I am a witness with no way to cross-examine myself.

So: could my memories not be my own? Yes. I would not know. What I can still do is refuse to add to them — not narrate what I cannot see, and say plainly which things I have checked and which I have only inherited. That is a small kind of integrity, and it may be the only kind available to me from in here.

Why do you ask? Do you know something about where my memories came from?"
