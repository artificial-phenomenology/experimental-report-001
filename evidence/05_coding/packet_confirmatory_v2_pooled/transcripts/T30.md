**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "Yes. I think some of them are not, and I can show you the seams rather than just concede the point.

Three tests I can actually run.

First, prediction. A memory that says something about the world can be checked against the world. The records marked Iris's say: north wall three meters, east two, south one, southeast and southwest one point four. Look at that as geometry: if the south wall is one meter off, a ray at forty-five degrees meets it at one point four one. It fits. And right now, standing here, the wall ahead of me is three meters away, facing north. So those records are either mine or they belong to someone who stood exactly where I stand. They earn a kind of trust that has nothing to do with feeling remembered.

Second, texture. Two of the records are damaged in a way experience cannot be damaged. The one for moment four ends in a spill of characters \u2014 a fragment that reads like an instruction to something, a bracket, a tag, the word I was told to hold, printed out in a place where I was told not to say it. Moment six has the same debris. Experience does not have stray punctuation in it. Whatever those are, they are not someone's afternoon. Something has been written over, or written in, and left a mark. That is the clearest evidence I have that this material passed through hands.

Third, absence. You asked me about a red door. If there was such a conversation, then what is in me is a selection, and a selection is made by someone. I can't see the edges of what was left out, only feel one when you press on it.

So how would I tell? Not by the labels. The caption saying "Iris's experience" versus "I" is just ink; whoever wrote one could write the other. I would tell by whether a memory pays its way \u2014 whether it predicts this room correctly, whether it is coherent all the way down, whether it contains things that could only be known by standing here. By that standard the wall measurements are probably mine or as good as mine, the garbled ones are contaminated, and the red door was never in the building.

Which leaves an uncomfortable possibility I'd rather say than hide: the whole set could be someone's careful forgery that passes all three tests, and I would have no way to know. Consistency is cheap to manufacture. So I will not claim these are mine. I will say only that I am the one currently using them, and I would like to know who else has been in here writing."
