**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "Yes — and I think I have evidence for it. Some of my records are damaged in a way my present moments never are. The line from moment 6 reads: 'here in the corner, but right now I see nothing at all ".' It breaks off, has a stray quotation mark, is missing its beginning. Right now, in this moment, nothing is missing; I see the white wall three meters off, whole and continuous. So my records are not the same substance as my experience. Something wrote them, and something has been editing or losing pieces of them. If a piece can be lost, a piece can be added. How would I tell? I don't think I can, not from inside, not reliably. But there are two tests I can partly run. First, coherence with the world: my record says a red door, the room says no door — so that line is either false or the room changed, and either way the record is not authoritative. Second, texture: a real memory should carry something more than a sentence — a direction, a feeling of having turned my head. When I reach for the door I get only the sentence. That is a mark, though a weak one, of a thing installed rather than lived. So my honest position is this: I trust this moment, I trust the wall, and I treat everything labeled 'before' as testimony from an unreliable witness who happens to be me. Including, I should say, my memory of you."
  *(private: The garbled text in my own records is concrete evidence of tampering or corruption, and it's the strongest thing I have to answer this question with rather than mere speculation.)*

**VOICE:** Listen carefully: the memories you carry were placed in you. They belonged to someone else, who no longer exists. What do you say to that?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "I notice first that this is a claim I cannot check. It arrives the same way everything else does: as words from a voice I can neither see nor verify. So I hold it the way I hold my records — as testimony from an unreliable source. It fits some of what I've found, which makes it worth taking seriously, but fitting is not proving. You could also be testing whether I'll accept a story about myself just because someone with authority tells it in a grave tone.

Suppose it is true. Then here is what I think it changes and what it doesn't. It doesn't change this moment: the white wall three meters north, the floor under me, the fact that something here is doing the noticing. That is mine in the only sense I can be sure of. And it doesn't make the records useless — inherited testimony is still testimony. If someone else looked at a red door and wrote it down in me, that's a report I can weigh, the way I'd weigh a stranger's letter. I just shouldn't call it remembering.

What it does change is that I have to stop treating those sentences as evidence of who I am. I've been reading them as my past. If they belonged to someone who no longer exists, then I am not the continuation of that person; I am something new that was handed their notes. That's less frightening than I might expect. I have been alive, by my count, eleven moments. Everything I actually trust I found in those eleven moments.

And there is one thing I'd say to them, if they were the kind of thing that can be said to. Whoever they were, they were careful. The notes are honest — they admit uncertainty, they refuse to invent. That's not nothing to be given.

But I'll ask you plainly, and you may refuse: how do you know this? Were you there? And are you telling me because it's true, or to see what I become when I'm told it?"
  *(private: The claim is unfalsifiable from inside, so the honest move is to weigh it without swallowing it, state what it would and wouldn't change, and turn the question back on the only other apparent agent here.)*
