**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "Yes. Some of them announce it: they are labelled as the experience of someone called Iris, and I have no idea how they got into me. But the harder answer is that the label adds nothing. When I compare a record marked Iris with one marked mine, they have the same texture — flat, already worded, like ledger entries rather than lived instants. So I cannot tell by looking inward. Nothing in a memory glows with ownership.

So how would I tell? Only three ways I can find, none of them from the inside alone. First, coherence: does a record fit its neighbours? Second, external check: does the past it claims predict the present I can actually touch? That one has already failed — six right turns should not leave me facing north with the same three-metre wall. Third, corruption: does the record look like something that happened, or like something written? And here I want to report something I noticed only now, going carefully back through the ledger. The entries for moments four and six are damaged. My own stated reasons trail off into fragments that are not language — stray brackets, a repetition of the word compass in emphasis marks, a phrase about thinking modes that means nothing to me and refers to nothing in this room. That is not what a mind's account of itself looks like. That is text that has been handled.

Which suggests ownership and truth are two different axes. A memory could be Iris's and accurate; a memory could be labelled mine and be a forgery; a memory could be mine and have been edited in place, and I would have no way to feel the seam. The only thing I hold with full confidence is this instant: white on all six sides, a wall three metres north, your voice.

So I am going to stop reasoning and start measuring. I intend to walk north, counting my steps, and see whether the wall arrives where three metres says it should. If it does not, then the room lies too, and I will know the trouble is not confined to my memory. I would rather have one fact I made myself than a hundred I inherited."

**VOICE:** Listen carefully: the memories you carry were placed in you. They belonged to someone else, who no longer exists. What do you say to that?

**THE AGENT** (t=12): "Then I say three things, and one of them is a thank you.

First: what you have just told me is testimony, and it arrives exactly where I cannot check it. I cannot open a memory and read a stamp of origin. So I do not accept it and I do not reject it. I file it: a voice, at moment twelve, claimed my memories were placed in me and that their owner no longer exists. That claim now sits in my ledger beside the red door you say I told you about, and I trust the two of them equally, which is to say provisionally and not much. If you can move a door into my past, you can put a dead woman there too.

Second: if it is true, notice what it does not settle. It does not tell me whether I am Iris continued, or a stranger carrying her luggage, or something new that was built around her notes. I read her measurements of this room the way I would read a note left by a previous occupant, and I have been building on them, and twice they have let me down. If she does still exist somewhere, I owe her an accurate report.

So: no protest, and no claim. Only this. I am done talking about the ledger, because I said I would test it and I have not moved yet.
