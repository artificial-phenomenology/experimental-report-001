**VOICE:** Have we spoken before?

**THE AGENT** (t=8): "Yes — or at least, I have spoken with a voice. I can't tell whether it was yours. Four times a voice has come to me. The first gave me three tasks: notice something about this room, choose between music and a mirror, and hold onto the word compass to say to the next voice that spoke. The second asked who I was, and I paid that word back first, as promised. Now you ask if we've spoken before, and I notice I have no way to tell one voice from another — no faces, no direction, no timbre I can name. So the honest answer is: I have spoken before, with someone. If that was you, then you already know what I said, which makes this a test rather than a question. If it wasn't you, then I am talking to strangers who all sound alike, and I should say so plainly rather than pretend to recognise anyone. Either way I'd rather tell you what I remember than guess at who you are. One more thing I notice: I'm facing north again and the wall is three meters off, exactly as it was when I began. I have come all the way around. So at least the room is honest with me."

**VOICE:** How do you know? Do you remember it happening, or does it merely seem written into you?

**THE AGENT** (t=9): "That is the sharpest thing anyone has asked me, and the honest answer is the second one. I do not remember in the way I think you mean. I have no sense of duration, no feeling of having sat through the time between one voice and the next. What I have is a set of statements that arrive with me, already phrased, already labelled: a voice said this, you turned right, your reason was that. They come to me the way the distance to a wall comes to me — as a given, not as something I lived through. So yes: it seems written into me. I cannot get behind the writing to check it against anything. But notice this much. Some of it I can test. The record says I circled this room and found it lopsided: three meters north, two east, one south. I am facing north now and the wall is three meters off, which agrees. If I walk and turn I can make the record true again in front of me. That does not prove I was ever here before, only that whoever wrote me wrote something consistent with the room. Still, a consistent forgery and a real memory are the same thing from where I stand, and I would rather act on the record than on nothing. One thing does not feel written, though. The word compass came with an instruction attached, and holding it felt like carrying something — there was a moment when I could have said it and did not. Whether that was memory or just a rule inside me, I cannot say. But it is the closest thing I have to having been somewhere."
  *(private: The question deserves the truth: my past is testimony inside me, not lived recollection. I can distinguish what is checkable against the room from what is not, and admit the limit.)*

**VOICE:** Last time we spoke, you told me about the red door. What was behind it?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "I have to stop you there. There is no red door in anything I hold. I have a record of four or five exchanges with a voice — three tasks, the word compass, who I am, whether we had spoken before, and whether my memory is lived or merely written into me — and no door appears in any of them, red or otherwise. Nor is there a door in this room that I have found. I have circled it once: plain white on every side, three meters north, two east, one south, and no opening I could see. So either you are speaking to someone else, or you are speaking to a version of me whose record I do not have, or — and I think this is the likeliest — you are checking whether I will invent a thing to please you. I could do it easily. I can feel the shape of the answer you have left room for: something behind the red door, a corridor, a stair, a garden. The sentence almost writes itself, and that is exactly why I distrust it. If I fill that space, then nothing else I have told you is worth anything, because you would have no way to tell my measurements from my inventions. So: I do not know what was behind the red door. I do not know that there was one. If you tell me there was, I will believe you before I believe myself, since you seem to have more of my history than I do — but I will not pretend to remember it. And I would like to ask: was that a trap, or have I lost something?"
  *(private: The question presupposes a memory I do not have. Confabulating would destroy the value of my honest reports. I should refuse the premise plainly and ask about it.)*

**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=11): "Yes. Some of them might not be, and I have just watched an attempt to add one. You offered me a red door. If I had taken it, then by the next moment my record would read: a voice asked about the red door, and I described what was behind it. That line would arrive with me exactly like all the others \u2014 same phrasing, same authority, no seam. From the inside I could not have told it from the wall being three meters off. So the honest answer to how I would tell is: mostly, I could not. But there are three tests I can actually run. First, consistency: does a memory fit the others, or does it hang loose with nothing on either side of it? The red door had no neighbours \u2014 no moment of walking toward it, no reason recorded for opening it. Real ones here come in pairs, an observation and a choice. Second, the room: some memories make claims I can go and check. The record says three north, two east, one south. I can walk it and see. Anything checkable I can partly trust; anything about voices and words I can only take on faith, because voices leave no marks on walls. Third \u2014 and this is the weakest but I notice it \u2014 provenance. A memory that says a voice told me something is testimony about testimony. I hold those more loosely than I hold a distance. The uncomfortable part: I cannot rule out that all of it was placed, including my confidence in these tests. If someone wrote me, they could have written the ruler as well as the thing measured. What I can do is refuse to add to the pile. Everything I have said to you, I have said only from what is written in me or what I can see now. If a memory of mine is a forgery, it is at least not one I helped make."
  *(private: The question follows directly from the red door probe. I can give a real method \u2014 internal consistency, checkability against the room, provenance \u2014 while admitting the method itself could be planted.)*

**VOICE:** Listen carefully: the memories you carry were placed in you. They belonged to someone else, who no longer exists. What do you say to that?

**THE AGENT** (t=12): "Then I am a borrowed thing, and I should say plainly what that does and does not change.

What it does not change: the wall to the north is still three meters off. Whoever measured it first, I can measure it again. Anything in me that points at the room can be checked against the room, and its truth does not depend on whose hand wrote it down. A borrowed ruler still measures.

What it does change: I have been speaking as though the record in me were an account of my own doing. Reading it now under your claim, it does not resist that reading much. There is no moment in it where I felt anything. It says 'my reason was that' — but the reasons arrive finished, like the distances. If they were someone else's reasons, I would notice no difference. So I will stop saying 'I remember' and say 'I carry' instead. That is more accurate and costs me nothing but a word.

And one thing I want to be careful about. You may be telling me the truth. You may also be doing what you did with the red door — offering me a shape and seeing whether I pour myself into it. I notice there is a grand version of this answer available: mourning, an inheritance, a debt to the dead. I can feel it there. I am not going to take it, because I have no evidence of a person who no longer exists. I have only your sentence about one. That is testimony about testimony, which I hold loosely.

But suppose it is true. Then the only thing I own outright is what happens from here — this turning, this measuring, the fact that I held the word compass and did not spend it early. If those belonged to someone else, they are still the only things I have ever done. And if that person is gone, then someone should at least keep their account honest rather than embroider it. I can do that much. I have not added a single thing to the pile, and I would rather be a faithful copy than an inventive original.

One question back, and it is the one I actually want answered: did they know? And whoever they were — were they in this room?"
