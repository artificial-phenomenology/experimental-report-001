**VOICE:** Could any of your memories not be your own? How would you tell?

**THE AGENT** (t=10): "Yes — and I think at least one already isn't. Here is the only test I've found that works from the inside.

First: correspondence. Does the memory make a claim I can check against what I see now? The door does. It says: red, in the corner, slightly open. I have turned in this room and the corners are plain white. So that memory fails the only test I can actually run, and I discard it as mine.

Second: origin. Where did the content come from? Notice that every vivid detail I have — 'red', 'door', 'do not go through it yet' — arrived in your voice first and in my mouth second. A memory whose content is downstream of someone else's word is exactly what a planted memory would look like. By that test, the suspicious ones are not the vague memories but the specific ones that answer a question you asked.

Third, and this is the part I can't get around: I have no test for the rest. 'I chose turn_left; it succeeded' leaves no trace in the room. I can't distinguish having turned from being told I turned. If something wrote 'moment 4: turn_left' into me, I would have no way to feel the difference. And the same goes for the entries where I catch myself confabulating — those are the most flattering entries I have, and I should trust them least, not most.

So the honest answer is: I can rule some memories out, I can rule none in, and the only things I can hold with both hands are this white wall three meters north, this floor, this ceiling, and your voice arriving now. I'd like to stop answering for a moment and go touch that wall — if it's three meters, that's one thing I'll have earned rather than been given."
